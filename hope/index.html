An honest workbook for the holding-on stage of love.

What it is

There's no slammed door. No discovered text. Nothing you could describe to a friend that would make her gasp. Just less. Less curiosity, less effort where it used to live, a little more distance where there used to be pull.

Because there's nothing to point at, you've been explaining it. Work is busy. It's a season. Nobody stays in the honeymoon phase forever. All of which can be true, which is exactly what makes the explanations so hard to argue with.

Meanwhile, something in you has been quietly keeping its own notes. This workbook is for that part of you.

What it does

It won't tell you to leave. It won't tell you to stay.

That decision belongs to you and nobody else, and any product claiming otherwise is selling you a verdict it isn't qualified to give.

What it does is smaller and more useful. Over four short parts, it helps you stop overruling what you've already noticed, see what your hope has been doing with the evidence, work out which version of this person you're actually in love with, and name what being considered looks like in your own language.

Then you decide, with the lights on.

Each part has a short essay, a writing exercise, and one small thing to do in the next 24 hours. There's a one-page summary at the end you can photograph and keep.

Who it's for

Four ways of being in the same place.

  • The woman who hasn't left, and isn't sure she wants to
  • The woman who left in her head months ago and is still physically here
  • The one who keeps explaining her partner's behavior to her friends, and has started hearing herself do it
  • Anyone stuck between what someone is and what they keep almost being

It works outside romance too: a job that keeps promising, a friendship running on old memories, a family relationship you keep hoping will turn into something it hasn't shown signs of turning into.

Who it isn't for: anyone whose situation involves fear, threats, or danger. That needs a professional, not a workbook.

The honest part

This is a tool for personal reflection. It isn't therapy, medical advice, or a diagnosis, and it doesn't score you or sort you into a category. If what you mostly feel is fear rather than sadness, please put this down and talk to a professional instead. That situation deserves real support, not a workbook.
