What it is

The text you drafted and deleted. The speech you deliver at 1 a.m. to nobody. The pettiest thing you noticed and told absolutely no one. The version of events you'd give under oath, and the version you give at brunch.

This is where those go. Unedited, unwitnessed, unexplained. Nobody reads it, and nobody gets to decide what it means about you.

The making progress half of the title isn't a promise. It's just what tends to happen once the loop is out of your head and onto a page where you can look at it. Not because writing is magic. Because a thought you can see is easier to put down than a thought that's still running.

What's inside

Three sections, and a tally in the corner.

  • The Drafts Folder. Everything you wrote and didn't send, said to an empty car, or rehearsed for a Costco run-in that hasn't happened yet.
  • The Admin of Heartbreak. The ruined song, the stuff still at their place, the mutual friends who chose, the algorithm that watched you pause on one photograph and built a whole personality for you.
  • Annoyingly, Some of It Is Working. The longest section, and the one you'll believe least at first.

Every entry is the same shape: a prompt at the top, the whole page to answer it, and one line underneath that does something with what you wrote. Not a lesson, and never a bright side.

There's a small line in the corner of each page for how many imaginary conversations you've had this week. Fill it in or don't. If you do, it slowly becomes a graph, and the graph goes down.

Open it anywhere. There's no order, no day one, and no streak to break. If you buy it in week three and open straight to the back, that page still works.

The honest part

This journal assumes your heartbreak is the ordinary, brutal kind. Sad, humiliating, survivable. If what you'd be writing about includes being afraid of someone, that's a different situation and this is the wrong tool for it. It deserves people trained for exactly that. There's a page inside that says so too.
